## The Gist {#gist}

Using GPT-2 contextual embeddings and inception-loop sEEG, we identify electrodes tuned to specific semantic dimensions on day 1, then test that tuning by embedding optimized words into custom day 2 stories.

## Q1 / H1 — Semantic tuning predicts selectivity, not always the direction {#h1}

**Key idea**

Semantically tuned electrodes show significant, selective responses to best vs. worst words — confirmed in 2/3 patients across all three approaches (LANC 1, LANC 2, NEURAL).

**Conclusion**

The direction isn't always activation. PAT_3780 shows equally strong, significant selectivity expressed as suppression instead — the electrode still discriminates best from worst words, just in reverse.

![PAT_6684 — best vs worst per electrode](/assets/photos/poster-inception-loop_results/6684_all_violins.png)
*PAT_6684 — all three approaches confirm best > worst at the target electrode, worst electrodes show null effects.*

![PAT_3780 — best vs worst per electrode](/assets/photos/poster-inception-loop_results/3780_all_violins.png)
*PAT_3780 — LANC 2 replicates the expected direction; LANC 1 shows a significant reversed effect.*

## Q2 / H2 — Anatomical distribution of semantically tuned electrodes {#h2}

**Key idea**

Despite broad, distributed general semantic activity across the brain, tuned electrodes converge on a consistent site — close to the classic semantic hub (ATL + pMTG, left hemisphere) — across all three patients and all three approaches.

![Combined significance map, all patients](/assets/photos/poster-inception-loop_results/combined_significance_map.png)
* semantically tuned electrodes across all patients — color = approach, shape = patient, shaded region = classic semantic hub.*

## Wrap-Up {#wrap-up}

**H1:** Semantic tuning predicts selectivity — confirmed in 2/3 patients across all three approaches — but not always direction; PAT_3780's reversed effect is equally strong and significant, just expressed as suppression.

**H2:** General semantic activity looks broad and distributed across cortex, but the specific signature tested here (best vs. worst word discrimination) reliably localizes to one region across patients — near the classic semantic hub — suggesting a hybrid picture rather than a pure hub-vs-distributed answer.

**Open questions:** why direction (activation vs. suppression) varies by patient/approach even when selectivity is robust, and why PAT_6684 showed unusual bilateral middle temporal gyrus suppression rather than the expected activation.

**Next steps:** extend the anatomical convergence analysis with more patients, investigate what predicts activation vs. suppression direction at the electrode level, and move from best/worst contrasts to continuous encoding models.
