My research asks how the brain turns spoken language into meaning, and in particular how
the bodily, sensorimotor side of meaning is represented while people listen to natural speech.

## Bridging Human and Silicon Meaning: LLM-based Semantic Targeting in the Brain
*sEEG · encoding models · naturalistic story listening*
<p> How does the brain encode the meaning of words during natural speech?
 To answer this, I developed an inceptionloop paradigm combining intracranial EEG (sEEG) with large language model (LLM) embeddings. In a first session, patients with drug-resistant epilepsy listened to a French audiobook while neural activity was recorded from 135–145 depth electrodes across the brain. Ridge regression identified which electrodes were most sensitive to specific semantic dimensions of word meaning — defined by their position in the LLM embedding space. In a second session, participants listened to custom-written short stories embedding words predicted to maximally or minimally activate each target electrode. Preliminary results show that semantically optimised words elicit significantly stronger broadband high-frequency activity (70–150 Hz) at target electrodes compared to control words, with significant effects clustering in left temporal cortex. This work provides causal evidence for the role of perisylvian regions in lexico-semantic processing during continuous naturalistic listening. </p>

## Sensorimotor language encoding

*sEEG · encoding models · naturalistic story listening*

<p>Words like *grasp*, *taste* or *whisper* carry meaning tied to action and perception.
I study how these sensorimotor features are encoded in cortical activity while patients
implanted with sEEG electrodes listen to stories. Stories were built and validated so that
each emphasises one dimension (motor, oral, internal or auditory-visual), with a neutral
baseline. Word-locked broadband high-frequency activity is modelled with ridge-regression
encoding models that use sensorimotor norms alongside controls such as surprisal,
semantic distance, concreteness and valence. </p>
