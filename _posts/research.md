My research asks how the brain turns spoken language into meaning, and in particular how
the bodily, sensorimotor side of meaning is represented while people listen to natural speech.

## Sensorimotor language encoding

*sEEG · encoding models · naturalistic story listening*

Words like *grasp*, *taste* or *whisper* carry meaning tied to action and perception.
I study how these sensorimotor features are encoded in cortical activity while patients
implanted with sEEG electrodes listen to stories. Stories were built and validated so that
each emphasises one dimension (motor, oral, internal or auditory-visual), with a neutral
baseline. Word-locked broadband high-frequency activity is modelled with ridge-regression
encoding models that use sensorimotor norms alongside controls such as surprisal,
semantic distance, concreteness and valence.
